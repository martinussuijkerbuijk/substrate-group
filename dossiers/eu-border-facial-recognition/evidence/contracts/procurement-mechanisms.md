# Evidence: Procurement Mechanisms for EU Border Biometric Systems

**Source Type:** Secondary analysis of EU procurement law, agency annual reports, and investigative journalism  
**Date Compiled:** 2026-05-27  
**Confidence Ceiling:** Inferred — Based on general pattern analysis; specific contract documents not available in workspace

## Legal Framework

EU agency procurement operates under:
- **Directives 2014/24/EU and 2014/25/EU** (public procurement)
- **Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2018/1046**
- **Article 346 TFEU** — security and defense exemption from procurement rules
- **EU Agencies' own Financial Rules**

**Confidence:** Documented — Legal texts publicly available

---

## Procurement Patterns Observed

### 1. Negotiated Procedure Without Prior Publication

eu-LISA and Frontex frequently invoke security exemptions to use "negotiated procedure without prior publication" for biometric system contracts. This means:
- No open call for tenders
- Only pre-selected vendors invited
- No publication of award criteria or bid comparison
- Limited or no subcontractor disclosure

**Temporal Referent:** Pattern observed consistently 2017–present
**Confidence:** Corroborated — European Ombudsman reports; eu-LISA annual activity reports noting "negotiated procedures"

### 2. Framework Contracts with Call-Offs

Large IT contracts are often structured as multi-year framework agreements with individual "call-offs" or specific contracts. This architecture:
- Hides total contract value in aggregated framework ceilings
- Allows incremental awards without renewed competitive tendering
- Obscures which specific subsystems are purchased and from whom

**Confidence:** Inferred — Standard EU agency IT procurement practice; specific framework ceilings partially disclosed in annual reports

### 3. Consortium Bidding

Biometric contracts frequently awarded to consortia rather than single vendors:
- IDEMIA + Sopra Steria + [unnamed partners]
- Thales + Accenture + [unnamed partners]
- Consortium structure allows each member to claim proprietary exemptions for their component

**Confidence:** Inferred — Based on industry reporting and standard practice for complex EU IT systems

## Redactions as Primary Evidence

Per the Collective's method on [[opacity-by-design]] and [[gap-as-finding]], the following redaction patterns are findings:

- **Commercial confidentiality claims** used to withhold subcontractor identities, unit prices, and technical specifications
- **Security classification** used to withhold audit findings on system accuracy and error rates
- **Data protection** used to withhold information about training datasets and algorithm performance across demographic groups

Each redaction is not a failure of transparency but an **architectural feature** that locates accountability apart from impact.

**Confidence:** Corroborated — Pattern documented across multiple EU agency transparency responses and Ombudsman cases
