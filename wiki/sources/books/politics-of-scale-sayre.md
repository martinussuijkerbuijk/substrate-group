# The Politics of Scale: A History of Rangeland Science — Nathan F. Sayre (2017)

**Argument:** Scientific knowledge about rangelands was produced at particular scales (the experimental plot, the grazing district, the watershed) that were never neutral — each scale embodied specific political and economic interests. Sayre shows how "scientific objectivity" in natural resource management has consistently served the interests of large ranchers and federal bureaucracies by naturalizing scales of analysis that rendered small-scale users invisible.

**Why it matters to us:** An empirical case study of how scale-choice in technical/scientific analysis encodes political interests. The parallel for AI is direct: the scales at which AI impact is measured (individual user satisfaction, aggregate accuracy metrics, national economic benefit) consistently make particular harms invisible while naturalizing others. The Material Ecologist and Counter-Forensic Investigator use this framework to ask: *whose interests does this particular scale of measurement serve?*
