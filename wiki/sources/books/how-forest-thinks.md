# How Forests Think — Eduardo Kohn (2013)

**Argument:** Thinking, representation, and semiosis are not exclusively human capacities. Kohn's ethnography with Runa communities in the Ecuadorian Amazon demonstrates that forests think — non-human entities engage in sign relations, interpret their environments, and constitute forms of meaning. An anthropology "beyond the human" must account for these relations rather than centering the human as the sole locus of significance.

**Why it matters to us:** Two uses. First, for the Material Ecologist: the ecosystems surrounding AI infrastructure are not passive substrates — they are sign-processing environments that AI development disrupts. Second, for the Philosophy Theorist: if we are investigating AI through cosmological pluralism (TEAM.md), Kohn's methodology demonstrates what it means to take non-Western ontologies seriously as epistemological starting points rather than objects of study. The Runa relationship to the forest is not a "belief" about the forest — it is a different mode of knowing that produces different evidence.
