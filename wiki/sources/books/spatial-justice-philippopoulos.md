# Spatial Justice: Body, Lawscape, Atmosphere — Andreas Philippopoulos-Mihalopoulos (2015)

**Argument:** There can be no justice that is not spatial. Law is always already spatial (it requires territory, body, jurisdiction), and space is always already legal (every space is claimed, regulated, contested). Philippopoulos-Mihalopoulos develops the "lawscape" — the tautological co-constitution of law and space — and argues that spatial justice is the struggle of bodies (human, non-human, technological) to occupy particular spaces at particular times.

**Why it matters to us:** The Capital pillar's legal-geographic framework. AI governance questions — where is the server? whose law applies? who has standing? — are lawscape questions. Jurisdictional arbitrage, which many AI companies practice deliberately, is a lawscape strategy: using legal geography to escape accountability. The "atmosphere" concept also matters for the Material Ecologist: the energetic and environmental atmosphere of a data center is a legal and political atmosphere.
