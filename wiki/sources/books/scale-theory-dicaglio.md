# Scale Theory: A Nondisciplinary Inquiry — Joshua DiCaglio (2021)

**Argument:** Scale is not a neutral analytical tool — it is a mode of cognitive and political organization that shapes what can be seen, compared, and acted upon. DiCaglio identifies three experiential origins of scale (distance/resolution, measurement/perspective, scope/accumulation) and analyzes how scale "tricks" operate to naturalize particular framings. Scale is always political: choosing a scale of analysis is choosing what relations become visible and which disappear.

**Why it matters to us:** Essential for the Material Ecologist and Philosophy Theorist when deciding at what scale to investigate AI infrastructure. A carbon footprint measured at the server level looks different from one measured at the supply chain level, which looks different from one measured at the planetary civilization level. The team's multi-scale approach is theorized by DiCaglio: no single scale is definitive, but the choice of scale must be made explicit and justified.
