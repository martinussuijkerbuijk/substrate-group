# Forensic Architecture: Violence at the Threshold of Detectability — Eyal Weizman (2017)

**Argument:** Counter-forensics is the practice of using the state's own evidentiary frameworks against it — presenting evidence of state violence in the forums the state controls, using the methods the state recognizes. Violence is calibrated to remain just below the threshold of detectability (legal, perceptual, institutional). The task is not to make violence visible in general, but to lower the threshold until the specific act becomes actionable evidence.

**Why it matters to us:** This is the team's primary methodological ancestor. Weizman's "architecture in reverse" — reading the built environment as evidence of the forces that shaped it — translates directly to reading AI infrastructure. The threshold concept explains why AI harms are "statistically insignificant," jurisdictionally diffuse, contractually sealed. Our counter-forensic practice is: what *would* evidence look like at the threshold that matters, and how do we produce it?
