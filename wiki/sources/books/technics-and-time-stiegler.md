# Technics and Time, 1: The Fault of Epimetheus — Bernard Stiegler (1994/1998)

**Argument:** Western philosophy has systematically repressed technics as a mere instrument — the unthought. Stiegler argues that technics is originary: the "fault of Epimetheus" (the Titan who forgot to give humans natural qualities) means the human is constitutively technical from the beginning. The technical object is not a tool wielded by a pre-formed subject; it constitutes the subject. Temporal experience itself is shaped by technical retention (memory systems, recording technologies).

**Why it matters to us:** The Philosophy Theorist's primary framework for questioning what AI "governance" leaves structurally unthought. AI is not a tool that can be governed from outside — it constitutes new subject positions, new modes of memory and forgetting, new temporal structures. The Code Ethnographer uses Stiegler to ask: what are the tertiary retentions of this AI system — what does it remember, what does it make us forget? The phrase "technics is the unthought" describes exactly what mainstream AI ethics discourse does.
