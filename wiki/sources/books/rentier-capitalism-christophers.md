# Rentier Capitalism — Brett Christophers (2020)

**Argument:** The UK economy (and by extension the global tech economy) is organized not around productive competition but around rent extraction from scarce assets: land, IP, financial instruments, platform infrastructure, and contracted services. Christophers argues that "rent" — returns from ownership rather than production — has made a "full-fledged comeback" as the dominant form of value capture in contemporary capitalism. Who owns the chokepoints owns the economy.

**Why it matters to us:** The Capital pillar's primary economic framework. Cloud infrastructure, semiconductor supply chains, AI model weights as IP, and public-sector AI procurement contracts are all rentier arrangements. The analysis shifts focus from "who built the AI" to "who extracts rent from the AI" — often a different set of entities. Key question the Capital Analyst asks in every investigation: *what is the rent structure here, and who is paying it?*
