# The Wretched of the Earth — Frantz Fanon (1961/1963)

**Argument:** Colonial violence is not incidental to colonialism — it is its structure. Decolonization requires not reform but total rupture; the colonized cannot become human within the terms the colonial system offers. The pitfalls of national consciousness: independence that replaces foreign elites with native ones leaves the structure intact. Mental disorders documented in the final chapter establish that colonial violence produces specific, diagnosable psychological damage.

**Why it matters to us:** Two uses. First, the structural argument: AI governance reform that works within existing power structures reproduces those structures. We investigate whether "responsible AI" frameworks are instances of Fanon's "pitfalls of national consciousness" — independence without rupture. Second, epistemic: Fanon insists that affected communities must theorize their own situation. This grounds the Community Liaison's role: community testimony is not data but theory.
