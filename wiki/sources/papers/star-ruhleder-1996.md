# Steps Toward an Ecology of Infrastructure — Star & Ruhleder (1996)

**Argument:** Infrastructure is not a thing but a relation — it becomes infrastructure *for* someone engaged in a particular practice. Star and Ruhleder analyze the Worm Community System (WCS), a large-scale collaborative software for geneticists (1991–1994), and identify why technically successful systems still failed users: infrastructure design must be understood ecologically — embedded in communities of practice, historical legacy, and uneven access. They introduce "boundary objects" as the mechanism by which heterogeneous communities coordinate without consensus.

**Why it matters to us:** This is the paper the Orchestrator cites first. It does three things for the team: (1) defines [[boundary-objects]] with precision — the concept that licenses the team's multi-layer method; (2) establishes [[infrastructure-invisibility]] as a theoretical claim, not just an observation; (3) the WCS case demonstrates that technical capability and community access are separate axes — a point directly applicable to AI systems serving (or failing) affected communities. The 1991–1994 window is also the exact birth moment of the internet as public infrastructure, which makes this simultaneously historical and foundational.
