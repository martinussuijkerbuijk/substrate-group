# Boundary Objects

**Source:** [[star-ruhleder-1996]], [[how-forest-thinks]]

**Definition:** Objects that are plastic enough to adapt to local needs across different communities of practice, yet robust enough to maintain a common identity across sites. They do not require consensus — they work precisely because different groups can use them differently while still coordinating. Examples: a map, a dataset, a standard form.

**In our work:** Infrastructure systems are boundary objects. A data center means different things to a logistics analyst, a utility regulator, an affected community, and a hardware supplier — but it is the *same* data center. This concept licenses us to work across evidence layers without forcing premature consensus. Each pillar investigates its own object; synthesis happens at the dossier, not before.
