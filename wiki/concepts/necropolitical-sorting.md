# Necropolitical Sorting

**Source:** Concept introduced in [[eu-border-facial-recognition]] dossier; draws on Mbembe's necropolitics and [[threshold-of-detectability]]

**Definition:** The use of technology to differentially allocate the right to live, move, and breathe. At the border, algorithmic systems do not merely identify; they sort populations into categories of risk that determine who is waved through, who is detained, who is pushed back, who dies. The technology operationalizes a sovereign power to decide who may live and who must not.

**In our work:** Necropolitical sorting directs our attention away from questions of accuracy and toward questions of distribution. It does not matter only whether the facial recognition system "works"; it matters who it works upon, and with what consequences. The team treats differential accuracy by skin tone not as a technical bug but as a structural feature of a system designed to sort the mobile from the immobilized, the citizen from the migrant, the living from the let-die.
