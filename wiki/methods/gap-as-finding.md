# Gap as Finding

**Origin:** Team method (TEAM.md). Grounded in [[counter-forensics]] and [[threshold-of-detectability]].

**Definition:** Documented absences of information are positive findings, not failures of investigation. When a contract clause is redacted, when a procurement record is unavailable, when a company's energy consumption is not disclosed, when affected community members report that no consultation occurred — these are findings about the architecture of opacity, not gaps in the dossier.

**Protocol:**
- Every gap is logged in the negative evidence register with: what is absent, from what source, dated
- Format: `[GAP — [description of absent information], [source], [date of access/redaction]]`
- Confidence level: gaps are assigned confidence like positive findings — a confirmed redaction is [DOCUMENTED]; an inferred gap is [INFERRED]
- The Shape of the Gap analysis synthesizes all gaps across layers into a meta-finding: *what is this system architecturally designed to prevent from being known, and by whom?*

**Why this matters epistemically:** AI systems are designed under conditions of permanent opacity. If the team treated every absence as a failure, investigations could never conclude. Treating gaps as findings allows the team to produce a complete account of what can and cannot be known — and to argue that the *pattern of opacity* is itself actionable evidence of intentional design.
